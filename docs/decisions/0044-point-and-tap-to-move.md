# 0044. Point and tap to move; the pinch splits into a tap that moves and a hold that talks

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-11-02

## Context
- The user asked to move the character to a spot they point at or pinch on, and to keep moving and talking apart. Until now the only way to send it somewhere was a voice turn ("come here", "go to the sofa"): the model or the local guard, an ASR session and, for anything but a short come request, a model round.
- The pinch already is push-to-talk (`PinchPushToTalk`, gated by UI hover and the palm-toward-head menu gesture), and the trigger is reserved: XRI's Activate and UI press, and a runtime can report a hand pinch as a trigger (ADR-0021).
- `ConversationController` acts on a press at once: it barges in on a busy turn, fires `PttPressed` (the "Listening…" caption), and `TurnListener` starts the microphone and the ASR WebSocket. Architecture §5.2 promises a pinch silences the character within 150 ms (the `barge_in` self-test checks it).
- Every locomotion tool walks with `ICharacterController.TryStartMoveTo` on the NavMesh (scanned floor, or the fallback disc of ADR-0025), and `SurfaceTargets` already turns a piece of furniture into the floor spot beside it.

## Decision
- **The ring is the affordance.** Every frame (`PointerTargeting.Tick`), the ray the user would pinch with (`PinchPushToTalk.TryGetAim`: the pinching hand, else the dominant hand, then the other when either hand may talk; only a free ray: no UI or interactable hovered, palm not toward the head, not hitting the character's collider) or else a Touch controller's far ray (`XrPointer`) is resolved by `MoveTargetResolver`. A hit at the character's floor height counts when the NavMesh is within 0.25 m. A hit on a scanned table top, couch seat or box top (within 0.12 m of its top, over its footprint) becomes the walkable floor beside it, on the edge nearest the hit (`SurfaceTargets.Approach`). Walls, the air and floor off the NavMesh give nothing. With no scene geometry under the ray, the ray meets the floor plane at the character's feet and the NavMesh still decides. A small light ring with a dark rim (`MoveReticleView`, `Jolly/MoveReticle`) lies on the floor there.
- **Tap or hold** (`TapHoldClassifier` over the pinch). At the pinch, the target under that hand's ray is captured (so the release, which jolts the ray, cannot move it). If there is one, the press waits: released within `JollyConfig.tapMaxSeconds` (0.25 s) it is a tap, held longer it becomes a talk press. If there is none, the press talks at once, exactly as before. So pinch barge-in keeps its 150 ms whenever the ring is not showing, and a stray short pinch at the air still opens a (short, empty) turn as it always did.
- **No word is lost on a hold.** `PreRollCapture` wraps the turn's microphone: a waiting pinch starts a local capture session, a hold hands it, with everything heard since the pinch, to `TurnListener` (the ASR then sends that backlog first, which it already does for the handshake), and a tap disposes it. A tap never fires `PttPressed`, so it opens no ASR session, makes no model request, spends nothing and shows no "Listening…". The pre-roll is skipped while the character is audible (Quest echo cancellation for the Unity microphone is unverified, research/voice.md §6, so it would record the character's own voice) and while a turn is listening (a second start would abort its session); that hold starts its microphone when it becomes a hold, as a press did before.
- **A tap moves; it does not barge in.** `PointToMove` never touches the conversation: a tap while the character speaks sends it walking and the reply carries on, like a voice-commanded walk that a barge-in does not cancel. Interrupting stays a pinch with no ring, or a hold with the ring showing (0.25 s later).
- **Controllers**: the trigger on the floor moves (`XrPointer`, bound by Touch layout name through `TouchBindings`, shared with `ControllerPushToTalk`); a trigger whose ray hovers UI or an interactable is left to XRI. A side whose hand is tracked ignores its trigger, as for A/X. The trigger still never talks; A/X hold to talk is unchanged.
- **The walk** uses `TryStartMoveTo` with a 0.1 m stop distance and `LookAt(User)`, so the character ends facing the user (ADR-0041). It supersedes follow mode or a running walk, like any locomotion tool. Feedback is the ring's 0.45 s outward pulse at the destination; no sound and no spoken line, so taps stay quiet and free.
- **Logs**: `evt=move.request source=hand|controller kind=floor|surface [surface=…] dist_m= status=walking|unreachable eta_s=`, then `evt=move.arrived ms= error_m=` or `evt=move.ended outcome=`.
- **Self-test** `tap_to_move` (after `voice_come_here`): `ScriptedPointer` aims from the head at walkable floor about 1.5 m from the character, a 100 ms scripted pinch goes through the real classifier, pre-roll, scene raycast, NavMesh and walk; it checks `walk=Arrived` within 0.35 m, no conversation state change or transcript, no spend, and that the microphone pre-roll started (`mic_preroll`). The scripted pointer aims nowhere otherwise, so the voice scenarios' presses talk at once as before.

## Consequences
- Moving no longer needs speech, ASR or a model round, and costs nothing. Voice movement ("come here", "go to the sofa", `walk_to`, `go_to_surface`) is unchanged.
- A talk pinch made while the ring shows reaches Listening 0.25 s after the pinch, and interrupts the character 0.25 s later than a pinch without the ring. Hand rays at rest often point at the floor, so this will be common; the words are kept by the pre-roll except while the character is speaking.
- A ring is drawn whenever a hand or controller ray rests on walkable floor. On the fallback floor it shows only within the small disc around the user and the character.
- One more depth raycast (meta-openxr's environment raycast) and one NavMesh sample per frame while a ray is free. Their cost on the headset has not been profiled separately; `perf_idle` runs with the scripted pointer and does not include it.
- Unverified with a wearer: the 0.25 s tap time, the ring's size (0.1 m radius) and contrast on real floors, and how steady the Meta aim pose keeps the hand ray through a pinch.

## Alternatives
- **Classify every pinch, ring or not.** One rule, but every barge-in would take 0.25 s more than the 150 ms gate, and the `barge_in` self-test would fail by design.
- **Start the turn at the pinch and cancel it on a tap.** The words are kept without a pre-roll, but every tap opens an ASR WebSocket (a billed session start), flashes "Listening…" and barges in on the character.
- **Barge in at the pinch, then decide.** Keeps 150 ms everywhere, but every tap cuts the reply short, and a reply cannot be resumed.
- **A separate gesture for moving (point with the index, or a double pinch).** No timing rule, but a new gesture to learn, and the pointing pose is already the vision tools' "this" (`IUserPoseProvider.TryGetPointingRay`).
- **The trigger for talk and the A/X buttons for moving.** Rejected again for the reasons of ADR-0021; the trigger is the natural "go there" on a controller ray.
- **Send the character onto the table top.** The NavMesh never bakes furniture and the rig has no climb clips (ADR-0025), so it goes to the floor beside it.
