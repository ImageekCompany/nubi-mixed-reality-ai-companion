# 0044. Point and tap to move; the pinch splits into a tap that moves and a hold that talks

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-11-02

## Context
- The user asked to move the character to a spot they point at or pinch on, and to keep moving and talking apart. Until now the only way to send it somewhere was a voice turn ("come here", "go to the sofa"): the model or the local guard, an ASR session and, for anything but a short come request, a model round.
- The pinch already is push-to-talk (`PinchPushToTalk`, gated by UI hover and the palm-toward-head menu gesture), and the trigger is reserved: XRI's Activate and UI press, and a runtime can report a hand pinch as a trigger (ADR-0021).
- `ConversationController` acts on a press at once: it barges in on a busy turn, fires `PttPressed` (the "Listening…" caption), and `TurnListener` starts the microphone and the ASR WebSocket. Architecture §5.2 promises a pinch silences the character within 150 ms (the `barge_in` self-test checks it).
- Every locomotion tool walks with `ICharacterController.TryStartMoveTo` on the NavMesh (scanned floor, or the fallback disc of ADR-0025), and `SurfaceTargets` already turns a piece of furniture into the floor spot beside it.

## Decision
- **The ring is the affordance.** Every frame (`PointerTargeting.Tick`), the ray the user would pinch with (`PinchPushToTalk.TryGetAim`: the pinching hand, else the dominant hand, then the other when either hand may talk; only a free ray: no UI or interactable hovered, palm not toward the head, not aimed at the character: a ray through its collider that enters it below the knee, 0.25 m, and lands on the floor within 0.5 m past its footprint means that floor, `CharacterAim`, any other through it means the character. The landing alone was not enough: at 1 m, a hand at 1.1 m aimed at the chest lands 0.6 m past, so the ring showed, a tap walked and a hold waited on a pinch at the character. Within about 2.5 m the floor behind the character now takes aiming beside it) or else a Touch controller's far ray (`XrPointer`) is resolved by `MoveTargetResolver`. A hit at the character's floor height counts when the NavMesh is within 0.25 m and the spot is at least 0.5 m (flat) from the user's head (`PointMoveSettings.UserMinDistance`): a resting hand's ray lands at the user's feet, and a stray tap there would walk the character into their legs. A hit on a scanned table top, couch seat or box top (within 0.12 m of its top, over its footprint) becomes the walkable floor beside it, on the edge nearest the hit (`SurfaceTargets.Approach`), under the same 0.5 m rule. Walls, the air and floor off the NavMesh give nothing. With no scene geometry under the ray, the ray meets the floor plane at the character's feet and the NavMesh still decides. A small light ring with a dark rim (`MoveReticleView`, `Jolly/MoveReticle`) lies on the floor there.
- **Tap or hold** (`TapHoldClassifier` over the pinch). At the pinch, the target under that hand's ray is captured (so the release, which jolts the ray, cannot move it). If there is one, the press waits: released within `JollyConfig.tapMaxSeconds` (0.25 s) it is a tap, held longer it becomes a talk press. If there is none, the press talks at once, exactly as before, and a stray short pinch at the air still opens a (short, empty) turn as it always did. Either way pinch barge-in keeps its 150 ms (below).
- **No word is lost on a hold.** `PreRollCapture` wraps the turn's microphone: a waiting pinch starts a local capture session, a hold hands it, with everything heard since the pinch, to `TurnListener` (the ASR then sends that backlog first, which it already does for the handshake), and a tap disposes it. A tap never fires `PttPressed`, so it opens no ASR session, makes no model request, spends nothing and shows no "Listening…". A busy turn was silenced before the capture starts (below), so a hold keeps its words there too. The pre-roll is skipped while the character is still audible, which by then is an interaction line in Idle (Quest echo cancellation for the Unity microphone is unverified, research/voice.md §6, so it would record the character's own voice), and while a turn is listening (a second start would abort its session); that hold starts its microphone when it becomes a hold, as a press did before.
- **A pinch silences a busy turn at the press, tap or hold.** With the ring showing, a press during Speaking, Acting or Thinking cancels the turn and flushes the speech at once (`TapHoldClassifier.Interrupting` → `ConversationController.InterruptBusyTurn`, then Idle), before the capture starts. A tap then walks and a hold then talks, and both interrupted anyway. First built, a tap left the reply going and a hold barged in only at the tap time: since a resting hand's ray often shows the ring, interrupting took about 0.4 s (the wait, then the 0.15 s fade of a move) against the 150 ms promise of architecture §5.2. In Idle or Listening the press touches nothing. `PointToMove` itself still never touches the conversation, and a controller's trigger, which never talks, leaves the reply going.
- **Controllers**: the trigger on the floor moves (`XrPointer`, bound by Touch layout name through `TouchBindings`, shared with `ControllerPushToTalk`); a trigger whose ray hovers UI or an interactable is left to XRI. A side whose hand is tracked ignores its trigger, as for A/X. The trigger still never talks; A/X hold to talk is unchanged.
- **The walk** uses `TryStartMoveTo` with a 0.1 m stop distance and `LookAt(User)`, so the character ends facing the user (ADR-0041). It supersedes follow mode or a running walk, like any locomotion tool. Feedback is the ring's 0.45 s outward pulse at the destination; no sound and no spoken line, so taps stay quiet and free.
- **Logs**: `evt=move.request source=hand|controller kind=floor|surface [surface=…] dist_m= status=walking|unreachable eta_s=`, then `evt=move.arrived ms= error_m=` or `evt=move.ended outcome=`.
- **Self-test** `tap_to_move` (after `voice_come_here`): `ScriptedPointer` aims from the head at walkable floor about 1.5 m from the character, a 100 ms scripted pinch goes through the real classifier, pre-roll, scene raycast, NavMesh and walk; it checks `walk=Arrived` within 0.35 m, no conversation state change or transcript, no spend, and that the microphone pre-roll started (`mic_preroll`). The scripted pointer aims nowhere otherwise, so the voice scenarios' presses talk at once as before. It picks only a point whose ray the real resolver takes for floor. When no such ray exists (`rays_blocked`) it degrades only with the head under 1.0 m above the floor, a headset not worn, and otherwise fails, since a degrade hid a resolver regression: on a keyed run with the headset on a desk (`user_h=0.71`), the point it picked for a character in front was walkable but its ray met something above the floor, so the press talked (no `evt=move.request`, a turn opened in the same 115 ms). The character played no part: `ScriptedPointer` never checks its collider and `ArWorldQuery` sees depth and NavGeometry only.

## Consequences
- Moving no longer needs speech, ASR or a model round, and costs nothing. Voice movement ("come here", "go to the sofa", `walk_to`, `go_to_surface`) is unchanged.
- A talk pinch made while the ring shows reaches Listening 0.25 s after the pinch, but silences the character at the pinch, like one without the ring. Hand rays at rest often point at the floor, so this will be common; the pre-roll keeps the words said meanwhile.
- A quick pinch aimed at the floor while the character speaks cuts the reply short, and a reply cannot be resumed. A user who wants it to walk and keep talking says so, or uses a controller's trigger. Accepted: being able to interrupt at once matters more.
- A ring is drawn whenever a hand or controller ray rests on walkable floor. On the fallback floor it shows only within the small disc around the user and the character.
- One more depth raycast (meta-openxr's environment raycast) and one NavMesh sample per frame while a ray is free. Their cost on the headset has not been profiled separately; `perf_idle` runs with the scripted pointer and does not include it.
- Unverified with a wearer: the 0.25 s tap time, the ring's size (0.1 m radius) and contrast on real floors, and how steady the Meta aim pose keeps the hand ray through a pinch.

## Alternatives
- **Classify every pinch, ring or not, before it reaches a busy turn.** One rule, but every barge-in would take 0.25 s more than the 150 ms gate, and the `barge_in` self-test would fail by design.
- **Start the turn at the pinch and cancel it on a tap.** The words are kept without a pre-roll, but every tap opens an ASR WebSocket (a billed session start), flashes "Listening…" and barges in on the character.
- **A tap that leaves a busy turn alone, with barge-in at the tap time.** Keeps replies going under a tap, but the 0.25 s wait put barge-in at about 0.4 s whenever a resting ray showed the ring. Chosen first, reversed (see Decision).
- **A separate gesture for moving (point with the index, or a double pinch).** No timing rule, but a new gesture to learn, and the pointing pose is already the vision tools' "this" (`IUserPoseProvider.TryGetPointingRay`).
- **The trigger for talk and the A/X buttons for moving.** Rejected again for the reasons of ADR-0021; the trigger is the natural "go there" on a controller ray.
- **Send the character onto the table top.** The NavMesh never bakes furniture and the rig has no climb clips (ADR-0025), so it goes to the floor beside it.
